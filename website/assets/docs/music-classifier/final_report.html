AI analysis

The model achieves 29.2% overall accuracy on 8-way classification, which is roughly 3.7× better than random chance (12.5%). However, this is far from usable in practice, and the per-class metrics reveal a highly skewed model. **Best and worst performers.** Rock (F1=0.418) and Folk (F1=0.446) are the strongest classes, with high precision (0.469 and 0.362) and moderate recall. Electronic has the highest recall (0.793) but low precision (0.267) — the model over-predicts it, likely because electronic textures (synthesizers, heavy bass) are distinctive but also bleed into other genres. The worst are Pop (F1=0.046, recall=0.029) and Hip-Hop (F1=0.093, recall=0.066). The confusion matrix shows Pop is almost never predicted (only 18 columns total) and is frequently misclassified as Electronic (69) or Folk (31). Hip-Hop is often mistaken for Electronic (172) and Rock (41). This makes sense: Pop is a broad, catch-all genre with no single acoustic signature, and Hip-Hop often shares beat-driven, synth-heavy production with Electronic. **Likely remaining causes.** - **Small CNN (16→32 channels)** has limited capacity to learn complex timbral patterns from 128×512 spectrograms. - **5-second clips** are too short to capture structural cues (verse/chorus, song-level instrumentation changes) that help distinguish Pop from Folk or Rock. - **Genre overlap** is intrinsic: Rock and Folk share acoustic instrumentation; Electronic and Hip-Hop share production techniques; International and Experimental are heterogeneous by definition. **Recommendations (priority order).** 1. **Use longer audio (10–30s)** and average predictions over multiple windows to capture structural variation. 2. **Increase model capacity** (e.g., 64→128 channels, add a third conv block) and use pretrained audio embeddings (e.g., VGGish, CLAP) instead of training from scratch. 3. **Address class imbalance** with heavier weighting for Pop/Hip-Hop, or oversample them. 4. **Use mel-spectrogram augmentation** (time/frequency masking, pitch shift) to improve generalization. 5. **Consider hierarchical classification** (e.g., first separate Electronic vs. acoustic, then sub-classify) to leverage coarse-to-fine relationships. The current result is a solid baseline, but without these changes, it will struggle to exceed ~45–50% accuracy on this dataset.

Pipeline

FMA mp3 → convert.py (16 kHz mono wav) → wav_to_mel.py (log-mel 128×512, first 5 s) → train_cnn_2d_light.py (2D CNN) → genre prediction.

Known bug fixed during this run: mel file ids are zero-padded (000002.npy) while fma_small_labels.csv ids are not (2). Raw string matching silently dropped ~56% of tracks; the four training scripts now normalize ids with int().
